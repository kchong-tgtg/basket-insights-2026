⚠️ Data quality — action needed from the commercial team (read before trusting product-level counts)

Product-level analysis here is directionally useful but not exact, because the same product offering is not recorded consistently when it's relisted. In just this 8-week NL basket window:

  • ~25% more SKU IDs than actual products — 628 distinct item_ids vs 468 distinct brand+name labels. The same offering gets a new item_id when relisted.
  • ~29% of products were relisted under multiple SKUs — 134 of 468 brand+name labels map to 2+ item_ids.
  • 4.4% of sales rows have no brand filled in — these collapse into vague buckets (e.g. "(no brand) | XL Besparingen").
  • Occasionally the name is tweaked on relist too (e.g. Chips Sticks "Chili Chip Sticks" → "Chili Chipssticks"; Lassie "Haverrijst Minuutje" → "Haverrijst") — but the dominant issue is simply a new item_id for the same product, so neither key is fully stable.

What this blocks: we can't reliably measure assortment breadth vs depth, or track a single product's continuity / performance over time (a product that "disappears" may just have been relisted under a new ID). Counts of "distinct products" are inflated by this churn.

The ask: introduce a stable product identifier that persists across relistings (and enforce brand being filled in). With that, we can properly answer breadth-vs-depth, product lifecycle, and repeat-purchase questions — for NL and every new market.

What to keep doing — NL & new-market launches
  • Curate €6–12 "anchor" products — high-ticket items like M&M's that land in a large share of baskets lift AOV more than adding items per order.
  • Find an anchor for each category — M&M's became the Snacks anchor; deliberately source the equivalent high-ticket hero in Drinks, Pantry and others.
  • Pantry is working as a category — keep investing in it. Pantry's revenue per order roughly doubled, with strong, broad-based performance across many products. Keep building it out.
  • Keep refreshing the range — most of the post-launch revenue (~66%, though our data can't fully separate brand-new items from relisted ones) comes from products that weren't selling beforehand. Continue introducing fresh listings.
  • Understand the Health & Non-food decline — both contributed less revenue per order than before. Worth investigating why and recovering it.
  • Monitor weekly — track each product's revenue vs all products and within its category, to catch winners cooling and confirm new ones replace them. (Tab 4 is the prototype; a standing monitoring dashboard can be built if useful.)

Methodology

Reproducible from one fact table, queried in Count (Redshift Serverless BPs).

Source, scope & windows

  • Table: tgtg.product_mart.fct_order_item_parcel — one row per order × distinct item.
  • Connection: Count → Redshift Serverless BPs (0sjxHt2lLsN), project Global Analytics.
  • Scope: country_iso_code='NL' AND is_basket_order=TRUE; date = paid_date_local; weekly buckets start Monday; partial week of 22 Jun excluded.
  • Inflection: week of 18 May 2026 (M&M's launched 19 May; AOV stepped up the same week). "Before/after" splits use 18 May.

Key definitions

  • AOV: total_price_incl_tax is the order total, repeated on every item row → one value per order_id (MAX … GROUP BY order_id), then averaged. Never SUM.
  • Category € contribution: avg of (category line value per order) across all orders, before vs after. Deltas sum to the product-value rise — the clean alternative to "share %".
  • Drinks price: overall average item_price_incl_tax of drink items (not same-SKU — the team relisted with different SKUs, so the right measure is the overall drink price level).
  • Gross vs net: M&M's "~€4/order" is its gross line spend; net is smaller because it displaced other snacks (Snacks net +€1.88). Category €/order figures are the net view.
  • Product/brand identity: brand_name (+ item_name), not item_id (item_id churns on relist).
  • NON-FOOD: parcel_type 10 = Personal Care, 11 = Skincare & Beauty, 12 = Household (raw parcel_type_name shows "UNKNOWN").

⚠️ Product-identity data quality (important limitation)

The same product offering is not recorded consistently across relistings, which limits any product-level analysis. Measured over the 8-week NL basket window:

  • 628 distinct item_ids vs only 468 distinct brand+item_name labels → ~25% of SKU IDs are duplicate identities of an already-listed product.
  • 134 of 468 labels (~29%) map to multiple item_ids — same product, relisted under new SKUs.
  • 4.4% of rows have a blank brand (collapse into vague "(no brand)" buckets).
  • Occasionally the name is tweaked too (e.g. "Chili Chip Sticks" → "Chili Chipssticks") — but the dominant issue is a new item_id for the same product, so neither key is fully stable.

Consequence: "distinct product" counts are inflated, so breadth-vs-depth and single-product continuity cannot be measured reliably (this is why the dashboard does not include a breadth/depth view — only a caveated text observation that growth looks breadth-led). Recommendation: introduce a persistent product ID across relistings + enforce brand population, so future analysis (breadth/depth, product lifecycle, repeat purchase) is trustworthy.

Other caveats: per-category/product € figures are gross of cross-category substitution; post window is a partial month; revenue is gross (incl VAT, no margin/cost); product "newness" is name-based (and subject to the identity issue above). Availability (in-stock breadth) rose ~122→165 items alongside AOV but coincident, not causal. Full SQL: project repo sql/q1…q11.
